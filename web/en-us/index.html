Distillation

A profile you do not have to write

Every dating app asks you to describe yourself in a box, on the worst possible day for it. Written reads what you already listen to, watch, plan and do — and puts the answer together itself.

  1. One

    Extraction

    One button per app, and never a password typed into ours. YouTube arrives through Google's own consent screen; Apple Music, Podcasts, Calendar and Health through the system sheets your phone already knows how to draw. What comes back is what you subscribed to, the music you kept, the evenings you booked, the miles you actually ran.

    Whatever a permission already covers is taken whole. Nothing new is asked for on a hunch.

  2. Two

    Ontology

    A thousand rows of titles are not yet a person. Each one is read against an ontology until it says something in a vocabulary everything else shares — so a Bach partita, a bouldering session and a ticket bought for November can finally be compared at all.

    This is the stage that decides what matters, which is why the stage before it keeps everything.

  3. Three

    Embedding

    Interests, preferences and temperament are placed on a space where distance means something. Two people who have never used the same word for it can turn out to be standing beside each other — which is the only claim about compatibility this app is willing to make.

  4. Four

    Dynamic prompting

    Every other app gives you one bio and hopes it lands. A profile in Written is read differently by each person who finds it: if you play the violin and the person you are looking at has a hundred interests including the violin, that is the one you are shown first.

    Nothing is invented. The order simply follows what the two of you already have in common.

Connected accounts

What Written reads, and why

Each source is connected by you, one tap at a time, and can be disconnected the same way. Written never asks for permission to write anything back.

YouTube

youtube.readonly

Read-only. Your subscriptions, liked videos and playlists say what you follow and what you keep — the strongest single description of taste most people have. Watch history is not available through the API and is not requested.

Apple Music & Podcasts

MusicKit · Media Library

The library you built, the playlists you made, what you have had on repeat lately, and the shows you keep on the phone.

Calendar

EventKit

The concerts, flights and exhibitions you booked, and the evenings you keep for yourself. Birthdays and public holidays are left out.

Health

HealthKit

Which sports you actually do and roughly when you are awake. These readings are derived on your phone; the underlying workout and activity samples never leave it.

Privacy

What we hold, and for how long

Limited use

Written's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from your Google account is used only to build and present your own profile inside the app. It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never given to a human to read except where you have asked us to look at something.

YouTube data, and 30 days

Written uses YouTube API Services. Titles, channel names and playlist contents are held for no more than 30 calendar days, after which they are deleted or refreshed from YouTube; what remains is the derived reading, which names nothing. Disconnecting YouTube in the app removes that data within 7 days.

By connecting YouTube you also agree to the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's own handling of your data is described in the Google Privacy Policy. You can review or revoke Written's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

What stays on your phone

Health is the clearest case: workouts and activity samples are read, reduced to a handful of signals on the device, and discarded. The raw samples are never uploaded.

Deleting everything

Deleting your account in Written removes your profile, your distillation and your conversations. There is no archived copy kept afterwards.
